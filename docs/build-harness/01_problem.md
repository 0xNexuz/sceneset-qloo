# Problem

Status: **VALIDATED FOR BUILD**

## One-line problem

Independent promoters and touring teams cannot reliably choose a culturally fitting market and local event program because audience taste is cross-domain, local, fragmented, and usually inferred through manual research or personal networks.

## Users and current workaround

- Primary: independent promoter, venue programmer, or artist manager.
- Secondary: brand activation and local partnership teams.
- Reviewer: artist/management approver.
- Today: spreadsheets, streaming/social popularity, search, local contacts, and intuition.
- What breaks: popularity is mistaken for fit; cross-domain links are hidden; recommendations are hard to audit; famous options dominate.

## Root cause

Teams lack a structured way to translate mixed cultural anchors into comparable, location-aware affinities across artists, places, destinations, and brands.

| Alternative | Strength | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Personal network | Context-rich | Narrow, non-repeatable, hard to audit |
| Popularity charts | Easy | Measures reach, not cultural fit |
| Generic LLM | Fast synthesis | May invent facts and lacks proprietary affinity signals |
| Enterprise audience research | Deep | Often costly and too slow for small teams |

## Success condition

A planner can compare candidate markets using the same cultural inputs, generate a local shortlist that respects declared constraints, inspect why each choice was made, and distinguish verified Qloo inference from unverified operational facts.

## Non-goals

Booking, contracting, attendance/revenue prediction, and proof of real-world availability.
